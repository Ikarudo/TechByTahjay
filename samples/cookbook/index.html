Brown Stew Chicken

Burnt sugar, pimento and a whole scotch bonnet that flavours the pot without setting it on fire. This is the dinner that ends every Jamaican week properly.

Prep 20 min Cook 50 min Serves 4 One pot

Ingredients

Servings
4
  • chicken thighs & drumsticks
  • lime, halved (for washing)
  • browning
  • dark brown sugar
  • ketchup
  • chicken stock
  • scotch bonnet (left whole)
  • scallion, crushed
  • fresh thyme
  • ground pimento (allspice)
  • onion, sliced
  • red bell pepper, in strips
  • carrot, cut on the bias

Method

0 of 6 done
  1. Wash and season, overnight if you can.

    Rub the chicken with the lime, rinse, then season with pimento, scallion, thyme, salt and black pepper. Cover and rest in the fridge, ideally till tomorrow.
  2. Burn the sugar.

    Heat oil in a dutch pot until it shimmers, add the brown sugar and let it froth and darken to a deep mahogany. Take it right to the edge of bitter, but never past it.
  3. Brown the chicken.

    Shake the seasoning bits off the chicken (keep them!) and lay it into the caramel skin-side down. Turn until every piece wears a dark, glossy coat.
  4. Build the gravy.

    Add the onion, reserved seasoning, browning, ketchup and stock. Drop in the whole scotch bonnet, and don't burst it unless you mean it.
  5. Simmer low and slow.

    Cover and simmer 35–40 minutes, adding the carrot and bell pepper for the last ten. The gravy should coat the back of a spoon and shine.
  6. Rest, then serve.

    Fish out the bonnet and thyme stems. Rest five minutes, then serve over rice and peas with the gravy spooned generously on top.
